Learning Blender

For this project, I modeled and rendered three everyday objects in Blender: a perfume bottle, a Moka coffee machine, and a peach. Each object pushed me to learn a different skill.

Perfume Bottle

Ideation

I chose a perfume bottle as my first model because it has a simple, elegant silhouette that felt approachable for a beginner, while still having enough detail to make it interesting. I liked the contrast between the smooth body of the bottle and the separate cap on top. In my sketches, I focused on the overall proportions and how the cap and neck sit on the body.

Approach

Since this was my first time using Blender, I followed a tutorial video to guide me through the process. I started from a basic primitive shape, then shaped it into the bottle body before building the neck and cap as separate pieces. Following along with the tutorial helped me get comfortable with the interface, basic navigation, and the general modeling workflow.

Challenges + Learnings

My biggest challenge was working without a mouse. Blender relies heavily on a mouse for navigating the 3D viewport, so even rotating around my model was difficult at first. I overcame this by learning alternative shortcut functions, which ended up making me faster overall. The key skill I learned from this model was the bevel tool. Beveling the edges softened the hard corners and made the bottle look much more realistic.

Moka Coffee Machine

Ideation

I chose the Moka coffee machine because it's a recognizable object with a lot of character, and it's more complex than the perfume bottle. It's made up of several distinct parts: the base, the faceted body, the spout, the lid, and the handle. In my sketches, I broke the pot down into these sections so I could plan how to build each one and how they would fit together.

Approach

I approached the Moka pot by building it in parts. I started with the main body, then added the spout and lid. The handle came last, since it needed to connect smoothly to the body. Working section by section made a complicated object feel manageable.

Challenges + Learnings

The most challenging part was the handle. At first it looked like a separate piece just placed next to the pot rather than part of it. Through this, I learned how to merge surfaces so separate pieces of geometry join into one continuous form. I also got more practice with the bevel tool, which helped the edges of the faceted body look more realistic.

Peach

Ideation

I chose the peach because I wanted to challenge myself with something completely different from the first two objects. Unlike the perfume bottle and Moka pot, a peach has no hard edges or precise measurements. It's soft, round, and slightly uneven. In my sketches, I paid attention to its imperfect roundness and the crease that runs down one side.

Approach

I started from a sphere and shaped it to be less perfect and more natural, adding the crease and adjusting the form so it didn't look too symmetrical. Once I was happy with the shape, I focused on texturing the surface, adding color to make it look like real fruit.

Challenges + Learnings

The biggest challenge was making the peach look natural instead of computer-generated. A perfect sphere doesn't look like real fruit, so I had to learn to embrace imperfection in the shape. This taught me how to create organic shapes and how to texture a surface. The peach only really started looking like a peach once the texture was applied.
